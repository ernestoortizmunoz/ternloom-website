## Evidence and interpretation

The seeds come from the approved website/services, plain-language buyer problems and bounded public search samples. This is an intent-led editorial grouping, not a measured search-volume or statistical SERP-overlap model. Monthly volume, difficulty and traffic remain **not measured**; no paid-tool data was available. Primary queries were selected for service fit and enquiry intent, not an unverified claim of highest volume.

## Cannibalization and maintenance rules

- Each cluster has exactly one canonical target; every CSV keyword appears once. Service overview, project proof and contact pages support the service targets rather than copying their keyword-focused body text.
- Commercial redesign queries stay on Website Development; the proposed redesign guide targets planning questions. The software service targets hiring/building work; the spreadsheet guide targets deciding when migration is justified.
- Informational pages should link to the matching service, one relevant approved project and contact where helpful. Counts are editorial choices, not search-engine requirements.
- Keep buyer language natural. Related concepts help explain the service; there is no separate “LSI keyword” requirement.
- Record a reason before changing a canonical target or introducing a new location page. A keyword-cluster spreadsheet is not approval to publish new services or pages.

## Measurement plan

For each existing service URL, track query impressions/clicks, selected canonical/indexing state and owner-confirmed enquiries by service. Use those observations to adjust priorities; do not promise rankings or attribute every enquiry to an unmeasured query. The website currently has no first-party visitor analytics; any future analytics or attribution change requires its own implementation/privacy review.
