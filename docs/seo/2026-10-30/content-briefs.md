## 1. What shapes the cost of a small-business website?

Primary seed: `small business website cost canada` (K07). Proposed URL: `/guides/small-business-website-cost-canada/`. Audience: an Ottawa or Canadian business owner preparing an initial website/redesign enquiry. Purpose: explain how to prepare a useful scope, without publishing an invented price range or promising a quote/response deadline.

Suggested structure:

1. Open with the decision: which pages, customer actions and existing tools does the website need to support?
2. Explain scope drivers: page/content structure, content readiness, approved photography, catalogue needs, enquiries and existing booking/payment connections.
3. Separate initial work from provider charges, domains/email, future content changes and any separately agreed support. Do not imply that a static website makes all hosting or maintenance free.
4. Explain redesign considerations: useful existing content, redirects, enquiry continuity and what must be carried across.
5. Provide a short enquiry checklist: business/customer context, existing site, desired change, content available, connected tools and known budget/timing constraints.
6. Link to [Website Development](https://ternloom.com/services/website-development/), the [approved project evidence](https://ternloom.com/projects/) and [Contact](https://ternloom.com/contact/).

Evidence allowed: Bonita Nights' booking-request flow and Wood Dust Creations' catalogue, Square checkout links, custom enquiries, blog and portfolio. Explain these capabilities only; no project prices, delivery dates, revenue or conversion outcomes are established. An owner-supplied real price example would need a dated scope and permission before publication.

Acceptance: a reader understands the information needed for an estimate and which ongoing costs need discussion. Distinguish buying a website from buying ongoing SEO, advertising or an assumed support package. Do not invent an FAQ rich-result benefit or add a word-count target.

## 2. Existing software, an integration or a custom tool?

Primary seed: `custom software vs off the shelf software` (K09). Proposed URL: `/guides/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf/`. Audience: a business owner whose current process does not fit available tools. Purpose: make the three options understandable before commissioning work.

Suggested structure:

1. Define the task, people involved, information required and what currently goes wrong.
2. Compare improving/configuring an existing product, connecting existing tools, and building a focused custom tool. Use a practical table covering fit, data access, permissions, review steps, provider dependence, handover and ongoing maintenance.
3. Explain when configuration may be sufficient and what would justify investigating a connection or custom workflow. Avoid presenting custom development as the default answer.
4. Describe a useful first version and how its acceptance would be agreed. Include data quality, migration and privacy as scoping questions, not promises of automatic transfer or compliance certification.
5. Explain that initial build cost alone does not describe long-term operating responsibility.
6. Link to [Custom Business Software](https://ternloom.com/services/custom-business-software/), [Business Automation & Integrations](https://ternloom.com/services/business-automation-integrations/) and [Contact](https://ternloom.com/contact/).

Evidence allowed: the approved service boundaries and Turnory's status as Ternloom's own Windows sim-racing telemetry application in development. Turnory is not evidence of a shipped client portal, dashboard or quoting system. Use any worked business-process scenario as an explicitly hypothetical example until a permitted real case exists. Avoid unsupported competitor/product comparisons, prices or time-saving percentages.

Acceptance: the reader can identify which option deserves investigation and what facts would change that choice. Keep commercial hiring queries on the existing service pages; this guide owns the decision/comparison intent.

## 3. How to assess a repeated data-entry task for automation

Primary seed: `how to automate manual data entry` (K11). Proposed URL: `/guides/automate-manual-data-entry/`. Audience: a small business repeatedly copying information between forms, email, spreadsheets and business tools. Purpose: help the owner describe one safe, useful handoff.

Suggested structure:

1. Map the trigger, source information, destination, responsible person and desired result.
2. Inventory supported provider connections and permissions; explain that some requested handoffs may not be possible with existing tools.
3. Walk through a clearly labelled hypothetical enquiry-to-review workflow. Keep approval and exceptions visible; do not present a made-up client implementation as evidence.
4. Address duplicate records, incomplete inputs, failed connections, manual fallback and who owns the next action. Monitoring and ongoing support need their own scope.
5. Offer a short worksheet for the first conversation, requesting examples with sensitive details removed rather than real customer records.
6. Link to [Business Automation & Integrations](https://ternloom.com/services/business-automation-integrations/), the [software alternative](https://ternloom.com/services/custom-business-software/) and [Contact](https://ternloom.com/contact/).

Evidence allowed: the service's potential deliverables and approved examples of booking-request and hosted-checkout connections. These do not establish fully automated bookings, inventory synchronisation, fulfilment or a custom payment processor. No guaranteed savings, failure-free processing or ongoing monitoring is established.

Acceptance: a reader can bring one concrete task, tools and review requirements to an enquiry. Explain limitations alongside the potential use. Review every claim before publication and keep the content independent of any unconfirmed platform specialization.
